# Station Density Roadmap

What gets built, in what order, and why. The research behind all of it is in
`docs/station-density.md` (2026-10-07).

| Phase | What riders get | Plan | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | "Biasanya padat jam segini" badge on rail station pages (web) | `station-density-plan.md` | none |
| **Data** | KCI weekday and weekend boards stored separately | `kci-weekend-boards-plan.md` | a weekend browser dump (user) |
| **2** | "Keretanya biasanya penuh" under each line's next departure (web) | `train-load-plan.md` | Phase 1 |
| **3** | Right levels on Fridays, holidays and cuti bersama, Ramadan weekdays, Car Free Day | `station-density-calendar-plan.md` | Phase 1 |
| Android | Phases 1–3 on the Android station page and trip board | not written yet | rebasing `feature/android-app` |
| TransJakarta | Halte crowding | not planned: blocked | see below |

## Suggested order

1. **Phase 1.** Everything else builds on its model and endpoint.
2. **KCI weekend boards,** any time. It's independent and small, and it fixes the planner and boards too, not just density. Do it before Phase 2 if possible, so weekend train loads start from the real weekend timetable.
3. **Phase 2 or Phase 3,** either order. Phase 2 is the bigger rider-facing win ("can I get on" is the question riders actually ask). Phase 3 is smaller and mostly protects Phase 1 from being wrong on specific days. Phase 2 starts with WD/SAT/SUN only; extending it to the calendar variants follows the same `VARIANT_BASE` pattern once Phase 3 exists.

## Android (deferred)

The app lives on `origin/feature/android-app`, which needs rebasing first. When it's back, the plan should follow the existing frequency path, which density can copy exactly:

- `CommuteService.getDensity` / `getTrainLoad`, alongside `getHeadway` (`core/network`)
- `StationRepository.density` + `observeDensity` + `cachedDensity`, mirroring `frequencies` (`feature/station/api`)
- `StationBoard.density: UIState<…>`, combined in `StationBoards.board` for **rail** stations (frequencies are TJ-only; density is the reverse)
- the badge in `StationScreen` above the departure items
- `PidsBoard`'s `PidsLabel.BOARD` state ("Naik di"): the boarding station's level at the ride's departure, plus the Phase 2 train level once it exists

Models are generated by fabrikt from `apps/android/openapi/commute-internal.json` (`pnpm --filter @commute/api openapi:internal`). That's why the Phase 1 and 2 schemas carry `v.title` + `v.metadata({ ref })`: they arrive in Kotlin as named `StationDensity` / `DensityHour` / `DensityRange` / `TrainLoad` classes. `DensityRange` is `{ min, max }` on the 0–3 scale (0 Lengang, 1 Ramai, 2 Padat, 3 Sangat Padat).

## TransJakarta (blocked)

Not planned, because two of the model's three inputs don't exist for TJ:

- **No departures per hour.** The GTFS feed lists the same frequency from 05:00 to 22:00, with no peak timetable. Dividing by it changes nothing, and the real peak buses would make load *flatter* than the rider curve suggests.
- **No demand per halte.** There's no published per-halte ridership, and TJ scores are all 0. Without it, every halte on a corridor gets the same level, which is a corridor curve dressed up as a station fact.

The TJ rider curve (JakLingko, ~9% coverage relative to MRT) is usable for **shape** only. It would be honest to show it on the halte page as a corridor-level hint ("koridor ini biasanya ramai jam segini"), not as a halte badge. That needs a product decision, not more modelling. Revisit when either input appears: TJ publishing peak frequencies, per-halte tap counts, or the user's own trip-mode observations on TJ.
